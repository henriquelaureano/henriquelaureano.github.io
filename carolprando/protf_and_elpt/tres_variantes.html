Overview & Methodological Background

This document evaluates whether serum PROTF and ELPT correlate with serum IgG dosage, the occurrence of infections (overall and by affected organ system), and survival outcomes (alive vs. deceased, cause of death, and time elapsed between exam and death).

Cutoff Methodology & References

The diagnostic cutoff boundaries and the Traffic Light System (3 Variants / Zones) used in this document are directly derived from the ROC curve analyses and two-cutoff optimization strategies established in the companion reports: - PROTF Cutoffs: Derived from respostas_protf.html using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis (pROC::roc) on non-replaced IgG sub-cohorts. - Red Zone Boundary (< 1.55 g/dL): High-specificity boundary (\(\ge 95\%\) Specificity) isolating definitive IgG deficiency with minimal false positives. - Green Zone Boundary (> 2.30 g/dL): High-sensitivity boundary (\(\ge 95\%\) Sensitivity) ruling out IgG deficiency with high safety. - Grey / Indeterminate Zone (1.55 – 2.30 g/dL): Biological overlap zone where PROTF alone is inconclusive and confirmatory IgG quantification is mandatory. - Binary Cutoff (2.30 g/dL): Primary single threshold (< 2.30 vs. \(\ge 2.30\) g/dL) based on the upper sensitivity boundary. - ELPT Cutoffs: Derived from respostas_elpt.html using ROC curve analysis (pROC::roc). - Red Zone Boundary (< 0.38 g/dL): High-specificity boundary (\(\ge 95\%\) Specificity) for ELPT deficiency. - Green Zone Boundary (> 0.60 g/dL): High-sensitivity boundary (\(\ge 95\%\) Sensitivity) for ruling out deficiency. - Grey / Indeterminate Zone (0.38 – 0.60 g/dL): Diagnostic uncertainty interval requiring IgG testing. - Binary Cutoff (0.41 g/dL): Optimal single threshold determined via Youden Index optimization (\(J = \text{sensitivity} + \text{specificity} - 1\)) in respostas_elpt.html.

All analyses below are conducted on the target sub-cohort of patients who have recorded IgG measurements and no immunoglobulin replacement therapy within 30 days prior to the exam. Hypothesis testing (Fisher’s Exact Test, Chi-Square Test, Odds Ratios with 95% Confidence Intervals, and Mann-Whitney / Kruskal-Wallis non-parametric tests) is formally conducted across all contingency tables, with Odds Ratios evaluated for event occurrence (Yes vs. No).

# Helper function to compute Fisher exact test with Outcome levels = c("Yes", "No")
get_fish_or <- function(df, group_var, outcome_var, exp_level, ref_level) {
  df_sub <- df |>
    dplyr::filter(.data[[group_var]] %in% c(exp_level, ref_level) & .data[[outcome_var]] %in% c("Yes", "No")) |>
    dplyr::mutate(
      G = factor(.data[[group_var]], levels = c(exp_level, ref_level)),
      O = factor(.data[[outcome_var]], levels = c("Yes", "No"))
    )
  tbl <- table(G = df_sub$G, O = df_sub$O)
  ft <- fisher.test(tbl)
  return(ft)
}

This section evaluates the joint, independent, and interactive effects of all four core clinical variables (PROTF / ELPT screening markers, IgG reference status, Infection history, and Death outcome) within non-replaced patient sub-cohorts.

1. Multivariable Logistic Regression Models (Predicting Mortality)

To test whether serum PROTF and ELPT provide independent prognostic value for mortality when controlling for serum IgG dosage status and infection occurrence, multivariable logistic regression models were fitted:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{logit}(P(\text{Death})) &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot (\text{PROTF / ELPT}) \\ &\quad + \beta_2 \cdot \text{IgG Status (Below Ref vs Normal)} + \beta_3 \cdot \text{Infection (Yes vs No)} \end{aligned} \]

Methodological Concept: Adjusted Odds Ratio (\(\text{aOR}\)) vs. Crude Odds Ratio (\(\text{OR}\))

  • Crude / Unadjusted Odds Ratio (\(\text{OR}\)): Evaluates the single bivariate relationship between one predictor (e.g., IgG status alone) and mortality, without considering other overlapping clinical factors.
  • Adjusted Odds Ratio (\(\text{aOR}\)): Measures the independent association of a predictor with mortality while holding all other variables in the model constant. It isolates the specific effect of that variable, controlling for potential confounding effects caused by simultaneous IgG deficiency or infection history.

Key Clinical Insights from 4-Way Joint Stratification

1. Compounding Risk of Dual Low PROTF & Low IgG

  • In the PROTF Red Zone (< 1.55 g/dL), 100% of all deceased patients (12/12) had Below-Reference IgG.
  • Patients in the Red Zone with Below-Reference IgG who experienced no infection had an 85.7% mortality rate (\(6 / 7\)), while those with infection had a 50.0% mortality rate (\(6 / 12\)).

2. Protective Effect of Normal IgG

  • In the Grey Zone (1.55 – 2.30 g/dL), patients with Normal IgG had low mortality rates (5.0% without infection, 8.8% with infection).
  • However, if Grey Zone patients had Below-Reference IgG, mortality jumped dramatically to 26.1% without infection and 46.4% with infection.

3. ELPT Pattern Consistency

  • In the ELPT Red Zone (< 0.38 g/dL), mortality occurred exclusively in patients with Below-Reference IgG (20.0% without infection [\(2/10\)], 31.2% with infection [\(5/16\)]), whereas patients with Normal IgG in the Red Zone presented 0.0% mortality (\(0/2\)) and patients in the Grey Zone presented low mortality (0.0% – 11.1%).

Summary of Applied Methodologies

This document applies a quantitative methodological framework to evaluate the diagnostic utility, infection association, and prognostic mortality risk of serum Calculated Globulin (PROTF) and Gamma Fraction (ELPT) in relation to serum IgG dosage.

1. Target Sub-Cohort Selection & Preprocessing

  • Sub-cohort Definition: Analysis is strictly conducted on patients with recorded serum IgG measurements and no immunoglobulin replacement therapy within 30 days prior to the exam (\(n = 138\) for PROTF, \(n = 81\) for ELPT). This isolates true baseline endogenous protein levels, removing treatment-induced confounding.
  • Data Processing & Wrangling: Data cleaning, string parsing, and dataset merging were conducted using dplyr, tidyr, and stringr. Temporal interval calculations between exam dates and death registration dates were computed using lubridate.

2. Diagnostic Thresholds & Traffic Light System (3 Variants / Zones)

  • ROC Curve Optimization: Diagnostic boundaries were dynamically established using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis (pROC package; Robin et al., 2011).
  • 3-Zone Traffic Light Boundaries:
    • Red Zone (Deficient Tier): High-specificity threshold (\(\ge 95\%\) Specificity) isolating true IgG deficiency with minimal false positives (< 1.55 g/dL for PROTF; < 0.38 g/dL for ELPT).
    • Green Zone (Normal Tier): High-sensitivity threshold (\(\ge 95\%\) Sensitivity) ruling out IgG deficiency with high diagnostic safety (> 2.30 g/dL for PROTF; > 0.60 g/dL for ELPT).
    • Grey Zone (Indeterminate Tier): Biological overlap range requiring mandatory confirmatory IgG quantification (1.55 – 2.30 g/dL for PROTF; 0.38 – 0.60 g/dL for ELPT).
  • Binary Threshold Strategy: Evaluated using Youden’s J statistic (\(J = \text{sensitivity} + \text{specificity} - 1\)) at 2.30 g/dL for PROTF and 0.41 g/dL for ELPT.

3. Statistical Testing & Risk Estimation

  • Continuous Correlation: Evaluated using Pearson correlation coefficient (\(r\)) and quadratic linear regression modeling (\(y \sim x^2\)) to capture non-linear relationship curvature.
  • Fisher’s Exact Tests & Odds Ratios (\(\text{OR}\)): Independence across 2x2 contingency tables was evaluated using Fisher’s Exact Test (fisher.test). Odds Ratios were estimated with 95% profile likelihood confidence intervals, explicitly orienting the event of interest as infection occurrence (Yes vs. No) or death (Yes vs. No).
  • Non-Parametric Survival Interval Testing: Time-to-death distributions across Traffic Light zones were evaluated using non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis rank sum tests (kruskal.test).

4. Multivariable Logistic Regression & Joint 4-Way Interaction

  • Multivariable Adjusted Odds Ratios (\(\text{aOR}\)): Fitted using generalized linear models (glm binomial family; McCullagh & Nelder, 1989) to test independent prognostic contributions of screening markers (PROTF/ELPT), IgG reference status (Below reference vs. Normal), and infection history (Yes vs. No) on mortality: \[\text{logit}(P(\text{Death})) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot (\text{PROTF / ELPT}) + \beta_2 \cdot \text{IgG Status} + \beta_3 \cdot \text{Infection}\]
  • 4-Way Stratified Risk Matrix: Joint cross-stratification evaluating compound mortality risk when multiple risk factors overlap simultaneously.
